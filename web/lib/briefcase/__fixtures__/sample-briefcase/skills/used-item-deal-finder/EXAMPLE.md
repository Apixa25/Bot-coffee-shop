# Worked example: used gaming PC hunt (Sept 26 – Oct 21, 2026)

**Goal:** a used gaming desktop for the owner, within about 100 mi of the owner's town (a small city in the US Northeast), up to $2,500.
**Screen:** 32GB+ RAM and an RTX 5070-class GPU or better (5070 and up, RX 9070/9070 XT, 4070 Super and up, 7900 XT/XTX, 3090).
**Hit threshold:** at least 20% below the new-parts total, and for a prebuilt also below its own retail price.
**Schedule:** Marketplace every other day at about 10:30 AM. Craigslist twice a day, 9:23 AM and 6:23 PM. Each was its own routine.

**What happened**
- About 1,040 Marketplace listings and 1,350 Craigslist posts were screened and logged in seen.json, so no listing was priced twice.
- The top 3 board at the end:
  1. Ryzen 9 7900X, RX 7900 XTX, 64GB DDR5, with 3 monitors: $2,000, 47% below new, about 115 mi.
  2. Ryzen 9, RX 9070 XT, 64GB DDR5: $1,800, 38% below new, about 54 mi.
  3. CyberPowerPC prebuilt, i7-14700KF, RTX 4070 Super, 32GB: $1,500, 17% below retail, about 25 mi.
- Sold listings dropped off the board automatically, and the next-best hit took their place.
- The owner ended up buying a new prebuilt (Ryzen 7 9800X3D, 32GB) at retail. The hunt's price benchmarks showed what a fair price was, so the search still did its job. Both routines were paused once the owner bought it.

**Lessons**
- Facebook ignores the search term if the URL has daysSinceListed or sortBy, so use plain search URLs.
- Craigslist's JSON search pads results with far-away posts, so always compute the real distance.
- New accounts get checkpointed fast. Browse at a human pace and stop at the first challenge.
- Long alert emails got cut off while being typed. Type them in chunks and check the first and last lines.
- Price estimates (RAM, motherboards) should be labeled as estimates. That honesty kept the board trustworthy.
